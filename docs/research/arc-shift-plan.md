# ARC//SHIFT first-player qualitative pilot

**PENDING REAL USER RESEARCH**. Actual participants: **0 provided**. The owner has confirmed no real player dataset exists yet. Everything below is a prepared study protocol, not an executed study.

## Objective and sample
Understand where a first-time player loses the ability to make an informed choice: onboarding, combat, weapon differences, protocols/builds, resonance, route planning, progression, difficulty and UI hierarchy. Recruit 8–12 adult new players, with a deliberate spread of action roguelite familiarity. These are recruitment targets, not results. Avoid a sample consisting only of project collaborators.

## Session structure (25–35 minutes)
1. Consent and context, 3 minutes. Ask about recent games and action/build-system familiarity. Explain that the game and tool are under evaluation, not the player.
2. Unassisted initial play, 8–10 minutes. Give the goal “start a run and make choices as you normally would”. Do not explain resonance, route symbols or upgrades before observation. Mark every moderator intervention.
3. Task probes, 8–10 minutes. Ask them to compare two weapons, explain one upgrade, locate the route's resource consequence and describe a build interaction. Use a fixed game version and task packet; record version/seed, starting resources and prior exposure.
4. Post-play interview, 5–10 minutes; survey, 2 minutes.

## Observation rubric
For each task record start/end time, unaided success, assisted success, abandoned, wrong mental model, evidence quote, action and confidence. Success requires explaining the relevant consequence, not just clicking the right button. Do not infer understanding from survival time or a random correct answer. Record accidental inputs and technical failures separately.

## 5–10 minute post-play questions
- What were you trying to do at the moment you paused or changed direction?
- How would you explain the difference between sword, barrage and cannon to someone else?
- Pick one card you selected. What did you expect it to change? What did you observe?
- What do you think resonance means? What led you to that interpretation?
- Why did you choose that route? Which resource or danger mattered?
- Which information did you need but could not find? Where did you look first?
- Where did the difficulty feel fair or confusing? Give one moment.
- What should the interface explain and what would you prefer to discover yourself?
- If one thing could change before you play again, what would it be? Why?

Do not ask whether the participant “wants an AI advisor”. Investigate the problem before the implementation.

## Analysis and decision
Import pseudonymous notes; split observation from interpretation; retain exact quotes, counterexamples and independent participant IDs. Cluster feedback, build evidence-backed insights, rank the top three problems, compare no-AI/rules/search/AI options, select one MVP and write an experiment protocol. In a small sample, report “qualitative pilot”, raw counts and disagreement; do not report statistical significance or population percentages.

## Data handling
Obtain separate consent for recording and anonymized excerpts. Store contact details and raw media outside Git in private-research/. Redact names, handles and other people's data. Default retention proposal: delete recordings 30 days after analysis unless separately agreed; pseudonymous notes may be retained for the project with consent. This is a proposed research procedure, not legal advice or a certification of compliance. Honor the agreed withdrawal window.
